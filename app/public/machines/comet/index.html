Comet

Williams, 1985 — shot-by-shot rules and strategy guide.

Open Comet in the Pinball Genie app →

Comet is a carnival-themed early solid-state machine with a short rule set and one famous shot. Barry Oursler designed it, Python Anghelo did the art, and it holds a small place in history as the first solid-state game with a million point shot. Everything feeds toward that: two target banks, a centre ramp, a corkscrew, an eject hole and a jump ramp with three rings on the end. No modes, no multiball, no wizard sequence — just a few features you learn to chain.

The layout

  • Duck targets — four targets above the left outlane. Clearing them lights the Corkscrew in the upper left.
  • Rabbit targets — four above the right outlane. Clearing them lights the Funhouse eject hole.
  • Comet ramp — down the middle, and lit only when both banks are complete.
  • Cycle Jump — a ramp in the upper right that throws the ball into one of three rings.
  • Dummy drop target — knocking it down exposes a bull's-eye for a short window.
  • 1-9-8-6 lanes — four rollovers across the top, with lane change on the right flipper button.
  • Left return lane — the only return lane on the machine, and the only way to light the Cycle Jump rings.

The banks drive everything

Ducks light the Corkscrew, Rabbits light the Funhouse, and both together light the Comet ramp. That third one is where the scoring is. A lit Comet ride pays 30,000 and then climbs: 50,000 on the second ride, 100,000 plus an extra ball on the third, and 100,000 plus a special on the fourth. The catch is that each advance has a time limit — miss it and the value drops back to 30,000, or the lights go out entirely once you have already banked the first 100,000. After four rides the whole thing resets and both banks have to be cleared again.

Repeat completions keep paying. A second clearing of both banks arms an extra ball on the Cycle Jump: the lower ring lights for five seconds, then the centre for ten, then the upper for fifteen, and missing all three simply restarts the sequence until you take it. A third clearing lights more extra balls and specials, collected on the Corkscrew — worth 20,000 regardless, so never a wasted shot.

Multipliers, and there are two of them

The playfield multiplier comes from completing the four 1-9-8-6 lanes, which also scores 50,000. It runs for twenty seconds, and the value depends on which ball you are on. In a five-ball game it goes 2x, 3x, 2x, 2x and then 5x on the last ball; in a three-ball game, 2x, 3x, then 5x. Because the lane change sits on the right flipper button, you can shuffle the lit lanes at any time, even with the ball nowhere near the top — get in the habit of tapping it constantly.

The bonus multiplier comes from the dummy. Drop the target, then hit the bull's-eye behind it before the target resets: first time 2x, then 3x, 4x and 5x, after which each hit is worth a flat 20,000. Treat this as a two-shot combination rather than two separate visits, because the window is short. It is worth doing early, because the multiplier applies to the Duck and Rabbit bonuses you have been building all ball — Ducks collected on the Corkscrew, Rabbits in the Funhouse.

The million

The Cycle Jump rings pay by height: 10,000, 20,000 and 30,000 unlit, or 20,000, 50,000 and 200,000 once the left return lane has lit them. On your last ball, with the 5x playfield multiplier running and the Jump lit, the upper ring is worth a full 1,000,000 — and the game will not let you forget it, announcing the million and lighting up the whole backglass.

The order matters. Light the rings first with a left return lane, because the multiplier lasts only twenty seconds and you do not want to spend it hunting a return lane. Then complete 1-9-8-6, then take the upper ring. The Jump also resets whenever the ball reaches the eject hole beside the lower right pop bumper, so you may need another return lane before your next attempt.

Small print worth knowing

  • Both outlanes score 10,000 and carry Special lights, and the left one advances your Duck bonus by three while the right advances Rabbits by three. Draining is not entirely a loss here.
  • The left outlane rubber can be nudged to send the ball back into play, which makes the left side less lethal than it looks.
  • A launched ball that never reaches the top and then drains without scoring anything is served again — but one switch closure and it counts as played.
  • Ducks and Rabbits are 2,000 for the first hit on each target and 1,000 after, so there is no value in grinding them beyond completing the bank.

Comet also plays very differently from one machine to another, and the reason is usually physical rather than software. Five adjustable posts around the return lane and outlanes decide how forgiving it is, and pitch decides whether the ramps are comfortably reachable: nominal is roughly six degrees of rake from front to back, with no lean across the width at either end of the playfield. Most timed features sit in the fifteen to twenty second range, though operators can change them. Glance at the outlane posts before your first ball; they tell you how much risk each ramp attempt is worth.

How Comet plays

Comet is an early-eighties Williams named for its roller coaster, a carnival of funhouse ejects, ring jumps and duck-and-rabbit target banks. Everything points at one last-ball prize: a five-times playfield multiplier stacked onto a lit upper ring for a full million points.

The loop

  1. Feather the plunge into the 1-9-8-6 rollovers, then work the right flipper button to shift the lit lanes and roll all four for fifty thousand and a twenty-second playfield multiplier.
  2. Clear the Duck bank on the left and the Rabbit bank on the right -- each lights its own collect, and finishing both together lights the Comet ramp down the middle.
  3. Ride the lit Comet ramp for a value that climbs with every trip, and cash your Duck bonus on the Corkscrew and your Rabbit bonus in the Funhouse.
  4. Roll the left return lane -- the only shot that lights the three Cycle Jump rings -- then aim high, because only the upper ring can pay the big one.
  5. On the last ball, stack the five-times multiplier onto a lit upper ring and the machine hands you a million.

Reading the lights

  • 1-9-8-6 Top Lanes (lanes) — lit: A lit top lane is one of the four 1-9-8-6 rollovers you have already rolled through; light all four and the game pays fifty thousand and switches a playfield multiplier on for twenty seconds. · then: Keep shifting the lit lanes under wherever the ball will roll until all four are in. The multiplier steps up over the course of a game and only reaches five times on your last ball, which is exactly why the million is a last-ball shot -- so know which shot you will spend the twenty seconds on before you finish the lanes. · control: Right flipper button: tapping the right flipper button shifts which of the four lanes are lit, and it answers even while the ball sits down by the flippers, so you can pre-set the lit lanes long before the ball ever reaches the top (any time, ball up top or not)
  • Duck & Rabbit Banks (status) — lit: Two four-target banks flank the outlanes: clearing the Ducks on the left lights the Corkscrew, clearing the Rabbits on the right lights the Funhouse, and finishing both is what lights the Comet ramp. · then: These banks drive nearly everything the machine does -- a second clear of both arms an extra ball at the Cycle Jump, a third lights specials around the playfield. Draining is not wasted either: the left outlane bumps your Duck count by three, and the right does the same for the Rabbits.
  • Comet Ramp (ladder) — lit: Unlit the centre ramp is worth ten thousand; lit by both banks it jumps to thirty thousand and then rises each trip -- fifty, then a hundred thousand with an extra ball, then a hundred thousand with a special on the fourth ride. · then: Keep the rides coming inside the time limit, because a miss drops the value back to thirty thousand, and the fourth ride turns the lights out until you clear both banks again. Take it at full strength from a settled ball -- a short shot rolls back into the middle and down the centre gap.
  • Cycle Jump Rings (shot arrows) — lit: Only the left return lane lights the three rings at the top of the jump ramp, and with no return lane on the right side, that single rollover matters more than it looks. · then: With the rings lit, aim high off the jump -- the lower ring pays twenty thousand, the centre fifty, and the upper two hundred thousand, the one ring that can climb all the way to a million. The moment the ball drops into the eject hole the rings go dark, and it takes a fresh left return lane to bring them back.
  • Dummy & Bull's-Eye (multiplier) — lit: Drop the dummy target and the bull's-eye it was hiding opens up for a moment; strike that bull's-eye before the dummy resets and the bonus multiplier climbs a step -- two, three, four, then five. · then: Play the pair as a single combination -- drop, then bull's-eye -- because the exposed window shuts the moment the target pops back up. Once the multiplier is maxed each hit simply pays twenty thousand, and that multiplier rides on every bonus you cash at the Corkscrew and Funhouse.
  • Corkscrew & Funhouse Collects (status) — lit: The Corkscrew in the upper left cashes your Duck bonus and, once you have cleared both banks enough times, an extra ball or a special; the Funhouse eject beside the ramp cashes your Rabbit bonus. · then: Build the bull's-eye bonus multiplier first, then collect -- Ducks pay only at the Corkscrew and Rabbits only at the Funhouse, and leaving them uncashed is one of the quietest ways to bleed points on this game. The Corkscrew is worth twenty thousand a ride and the Funhouse ten even unlit, so neither is a wasted shot.

Lane change: the flipper buttons move the lit lane. The right flipper button shifts which of the four 1-9-8-6 lanes are lit, and it answers even when the ball is down by the flippers, so you can pre-set the lit lanes before the ball ever reaches the top. Roll all four to score fifty thousand and start a twenty-second playfield multiplier -- and since that multiplier only reaches five times on your last ball, this lane change is the setup for the million-point shot.

Start scoring

  • Ball one — Take the safe carnival points first. Feather a plunge into the rollover lanes, clear the Duck and Rabbit banks since they light almost everything on the machine, and start stepping the bonus multiplier up at the dummy and bull's-eye. — Plunge Into The Lanes, The Duck Bank, The Rabbit Bank, Dunk The Dummy
  • Building — Complete 1-9-8-6 for the playfield multiplier and know where its twenty seconds will go. Ride the Comet ramp up its ladder, feed the left return lane so the Cycle Jump rings light, and collect your Duck and Rabbit bonuses at full multiplier. — Light 1-9-8-6, Ride The Comet, The Cycle Jump, Collect Your Bonuses
  • The big one — The whole machine is built for one shot: the million. On the last ball the playfield multiplier reaches five times, so light the Cycle Jump rings from the left return lane, complete 1-9-8-6 to turn on the 5x, and land the upper ring before the twenty seconds run out. — The Million Point Shot, The Cycle Jump, Light 1-9-8-6

Does Comet have an action button?

Nothing dedicated on the cabinet — flippers, a start button and a manual plunger. The right flipper button does pull double duty as lane change, and that is the closest thing to an extra control you get.

Goals in Comet

Every goal below is tracked step-by-step, with shot paths drawn on the playfield map, inside the app.

  • Plunge Into The Lanes skillshot · Easy

    Your plunge wants just enough steam to park the ball in the pocket above the top lanes, so that it rolls back down through one of them. Do that and the pop bumpers take over, eventually spitting the ball out on the left of the Comet ramp or on the right. Over-plunge and none of it happens: the Corkscrew catches the ball and delivers it back to your flippers.

    Reward: A lit lane and a controlled entry

  • Light 1-9-8-6 multiplier · Medium

    Completing the four top lanes scores 50,000 and switches on the playfield multiplier for twenty seconds. Which multiplier you get depends on which ball you are on, and the last ball is the big one — this is the single most important habit to build on this machine.

    Reward: 50,000 and a 20-second playfield multiplier

  • The Duck Bank progression · Easy

    Four targets above the left outlane. Clearing the bank lights the Corkscrew, and the left outlane pushes your Duck bonus up by three every time you drain there. The first hit on each duck is worth 2,000 and every hit after that is worth 1,000.

    Reward: Lights the Corkscrew

  • The Rabbit Bank progression · Easy

    The mirror image on the right. Four targets above the right outlane, and clearing them lights the Funhouse eject hole to collect your Rabbit bonus. The right outlane advances that bonus by three on a drain.

    Reward: Lights the Funhouse

  • Ride The Comet progression · Medium

    Complete both the Duck and Rabbit banks and the centre ramp lights up. Unlit it is 10,000; lit it pays 30,000 and then climbs — 50,000 on the second ride, 100,000 and an extra ball on the third, 100,000 and a special on the fourth.

    Reward: Up to 100,000 a ride, plus an extra ball and a special

  • Dunk The Dummy multiplier · Medium

    Knock the dummy drop target over and the bull's-eye behind it is exposed for a short window. Hit the bull's-eye before the target resets and your bonus multiplier steps up — 2x, then 3x, 4x and 5x. After that each hit is simply worth 20,000.

    Reward: Bonus multiplier up to 5x, then 20,000 a hit

  • The Cycle Jump strategy · Medium

    Three rings sit at the end of the jump ramp and they pay by height: 10,000 for the lower, 20,000 for the centre, 30,000 for the upper — or 20,000, 50,000 and 200,000 with the rings lit. The left return lane is the only thing that lights them.

    Reward: Up to 200,000 for the upper ring

  • The Million Point Shot wizard · Hard

    The reason this machine exists. On your last ball, complete 1-9-8-6 for the 5x playfield multiplier, have the Cycle Jump lit, and land the upper ring before the multiplier expires. The game announces it, the backglass goes wild, and you have a million points.

    Reward: 1,000,000

  • The Cycle Jump Extra Ball strategy · Hard

    Complete both target banks a second time and the Cycle Jump starts a timed ladder for an extra ball: the lower ring lights for five seconds, then the centre for ten, then the upper for fifteen. Miss all three and the sequence simply starts again.

    Reward: Extra ball

  • The Corkscrew Special strategy · Hard

    A third completion of both banks lights more extra balls and specials around the playfield, and the Corkscrew becomes the place to cash them. It is worth 20,000 a ride regardless, so it is never a wasted shot.

    Reward: Special or extra ball at the Corkscrew

  • Make The Outlanes Pay strategy · Medium

    Draining is not free of value here. Each outlane is worth 10,000, the left one pushes your Duck bonus up by three and the right one does the same for Rabbits, and both carry Special lights. The left outlane will occasionally give the ball back off its rubber if you nudge well.

    Reward: 10,000, three bonus advances, and a chance at a Special

  • Collect Your Bonuses strategy · Medium

    The two bonus counters are collected in two different places, and forgetting to cash them is the most common way to leave points behind. Ducks are collected on the Corkscrew, Rabbits in the Funhouse, and the bull's-eye multiplier applies to all of it.

    Reward: Bonus x up to 5

  • The Ball That Never Started secret · Easy

    A merciful little rule. If a launched ball fails to reach the top of the playfield, comes back down and drains without scoring a single point, the machine serves it again. It only applies to that specific case — a ball that scored anything at all is gone.

    Reward: The ball back

  • How The Machine Is Set Up secret · Easy

    Comet plays very differently from one machine to the next, and the difference is mostly physical. Five adjustable posts around the return lane and outlanes decide how liberal the game is, and pitch decides whether the ramps are reachable at all.

    Reward: Knowing what you are up against
